# Shared comparison passage (~70–80 seconds spoken)
# Used for all voice A/B samples so comparison is fair.

You’ve probably seen the morning routine that looks perfect on a phone.

Wake at five. Journal. Train. Cold shower. Win the day.

Then the roster moves.

You finish late. Overtime lands. A kid’s up at two. By Thursday that neat plan is gone, and it’s easy to decide the problem is you.

Usually, it isn’t.

Most of that advice assumes a life a lot of shift workers don’t have. Same start time. Quiet house. Decent sleep. A calendar that stays put. If your week doesn’t look like that, you don’t need another pep talk. You need a standard that still works when the week gets messy.

That’s what the five a.m. standard is. It’s not a religion about the number on the clock. It’s a practical rule: protect the first block of your day before everyone else’s priorities take over.

That block might start at five. It might start at twenty to six. After nights, it might start at two in the afternoon. You can still call it your morning, because it’s the first stretch you actually control after you wake.

And if you’re too wrecked to drive safely, sleep. The standard is meant to support a working life. It isn’t meant to compete with basic safety.
