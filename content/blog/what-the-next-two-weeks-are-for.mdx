You can be busy for a fortnight and still not know what it was for. The calendar fills. The loudest request wins. At the end you are tired, and the thing you meant to move has not moved.

You do not need a life purpose before Thursday. You need one honest answer to a smaller question: what are the next two weeks for?

One thing. Fourteen days. Then you look again.

## Pick one, and say it in a sentence

Write a sentence a stranger could understand.

Useful:

- “These two weeks are for getting the backyard quote and booking the job.”
- “These two weeks are for being home for dinner four nights, because I have been missing them.”
- “These two weeks are for finishing the certificate module I keep opening and closing.”

Less useful:

- “Be better.”
- “Sort my life.”
- “Focus on what matters.”

If you have three sentences, you do not have a direction yet. You have a wish list. Choose the one that would still matter if the other two waited until the fortnight after.

This is not a vow about the kind of man you are. It is a use of the next fourteen days.

## Name what you will disappoint

A direction that does not disappoint anyone is usually a slogan. Time given here is time taken from somewhere else.

Write the no beside the yes.

- If the fortnight is for the certificate, you do not take the extra shift that would eat the two evenings you need.
- If the fortnight is for dinners at home, you do not say yes to a mate’s project that needs you at 7pm.
- If the fortnight is for the quote and the booking, you do not also repaint the spare room “while you’re at it.”

Saying no can be warm and still be clear. “I can look at that on the 14th. These two weeks are already spoken for.”

If a no would break a promise you have already made to your partner, your child, or your boss, the new aim is the thing that waits. [Keep Your Word](/blog/keep-your-word-honour-commitments) comes before a fresh plan.

## Put it on real days

An aim with no day attached will lose to whatever pings you at 8pm.

For a fortnight, four short sittings are enough to prove the sentence is real. Not a heroic Saturday that never arrives.

Checked arithmetic, with the assumptions stated:

- Four evenings of 45 minutes is 180 minutes, which is 3 hours.
- One Saturday morning of 90 minutes brings it to 4 and a half hours across fourteen days.
- That is not a new career. It is enough to finish a module, get two quotes, or be at the table four nights instead of one.

Put the four evenings in the calendar before you tell anyone the sentence. If you cannot find four, the aim is too big for this fortnight. Shrink it until it fits. “Get one quote,” not “renovate.”

If a week inside the fortnight turns heavy, do not scrap the sentence. Do the smallest piece and keep the date. [When the Week Is Already Heavy](/blog/when-the-week-is-already-heavy) is how to stay useful without pretending the load is not there. Missing one evening is not a reason to throw out the other three.

## An illustrative fortnight

Assume a man who wants to change jobs and also feels he is missing his kids, and also has a leaking tap. He wants all three to be “the focus.”

He writes them down and chooses one: be home for dinner four nights in the next fourteen days. The tap gets a plumber booked for the fortnight after, one phone call, not a weekend project. The job search gets one hour on Sunday to update the résumé, and then it waits. Assumption: his partner wants him at those dinners. If they need him on the nights for something else, he asks which four nights actually help, instead of announcing a plan.

He puts Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, and the following Monday in the diary. Each is from 6:15pm. He tells his boss on Monday morning that he is leaving on time those days. One of the Tuesdays blows out. He does not add a speech. He keeps Thursday, and he says at breakfast that Tuesday moved.

At the end of the fourteen days he has been home three nights, not four, and the plumber is booked. That is a fortnight with a direction. It is not a new life.

## Leave these alone

Do not wait for a quiet week before you choose. Quiet weeks are rare, and the loud ones are where the sentence is tested.

Do not pick a direction that needs you to become a different man by Friday. Pick one that the man you are this month can do.

Do not announce it widely. One person who shares the house, or the calendar, is enough. A public launch makes the aim about being seen.

## Keep this

Write one sentence for the next fourteen days.

Write the no that makes the sentence possible.

Put four real times in the diary. If you cannot find them, make the sentence smaller until you can.
