You can be busy for a fortnight and still not know what it was for. The calendar fills. The loudest request wins. At the end you are tired, and the thing you meant to move has not moved.

You do not need a life purpose before Thursday. You need one honest answer to a smaller question. What are the next two weeks for?

One thing. Fourteen days. Then you look again.

Write a sentence a stranger could understand. These two weeks are for getting the backyard quote and booking the job. Or for being home for dinner four nights. Or for finishing the certificate module you keep opening and closing.

Be better, sort my life, and focus on what matters are not sentences. They are wishes.

If you have three sentences, you do not have a direction yet. Choose the one that would still matter if the other two waited.

A direction that does not disappoint anyone is usually a slogan. Time given here is time taken from somewhere else. Write the no beside the yes. If the fortnight is for the certificate, you do not take the extra shift that would eat the two evenings you need. If it is for dinners at home, you do not say yes to a project that needs you at seven. If a no would break a promise you have already made, the new aim is the thing that waits.

Put the work on real days. Four short sittings are enough. Four evenings of forty-five minutes is three hours. One Saturday morning of ninety minutes brings it to four and a half hours across fourteen days. That is not a new career. It is enough to finish a module, get two quotes, or be at the table four nights instead of one.

Put those times in the calendar before you tell anyone. If you cannot find four, the aim is too big. Shrink it.

If a week inside the fortnight turns heavy, do not scrap the sentence. Do the smallest piece and keep the date. Missing one evening is not a reason to throw out the other three.

Picture a man who wants to change jobs, and also feels he is missing his kids, and also has a leaking tap. He chooses one. Be home for dinner four nights. The tap gets a plumber booked for later, one phone call. The job search gets one hour on Sunday, and then it waits. He asks which nights actually help, instead of announcing a plan. One night blows out. He keeps the next one.

At the end he has been home three nights, not four, and the plumber is booked. That is a fortnight with a direction. It is not a new life.

Do not wait for a quiet week. Do not pick a direction that needs you to become a different man by Friday. Do not announce it widely.

Write one sentence for the next fourteen days. Write the no that makes it possible. Put four real times in the diary. If you cannot find them, make the sentence smaller until you can.
