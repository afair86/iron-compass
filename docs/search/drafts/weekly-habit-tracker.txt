Proposed title: How to Track Weekly Habits When Your Days Change
Proposed address: /blog/weekly-habit-tracker
Primary cluster: weekly habit tracker / flexible habit tracker

A weekly habit target asks how often you will act during the week. If you agree
to one jiu-jitsu session and Tuesday falls through, completing it on Wednesday
still fulfils that week's commitment. The planned day helps you make room for
the action; the weekly target defines completion.

DAILY AND WEEKLY HABITS NEED DIFFERENT RECORDS
Some actions belong to every day. Others, such as a class, a longer training
session or a weekly household review, do not. Giving a once-a-week action seven
daily boxes can make a completed week look like six failures. Write the actual
frequency beside the habit before choosing days.

CHOOSE THE TARGET, THEN THE OPPORTUNITIES
Start with the responsibility and a realistic number of days. Name what counts
as a completed action. Choose likely opportunities and one fallback. Do not
set the frequency from the week you wish you had; check work, caring duties,
travel and recovery first.

Illustrative example: three training days within a Monday-to-Sunday week.
Monday and Friday are completed. Wednesday becomes impossible. Saturday is
available without cutting necessary rest or shifting an unagreed duty to
someone else. Completing Saturday makes three. The week passes against the
agreed target, even though the original day changed.

If Saturday is unavailable too, record two out of three honestly. Decide what
needs changing next week. Do not mark a session you did not do or relabel an
unrelated activity to protect the record.

A PAPER CHECKLIST
Habit:
What counts as completion:
Target days this week:
Likely opportunities:
Fallback opportunity:
Actual completion dates:
What changed:
Next week's decision:

This works in a notebook without an account. Keep the week's start and end
consistent. Do not count a Sunday session in both weeks. One completion on a
calendar day counts once for that habit; multiple-session sports tracking is
a separate measurement and should not be implied by this daily check-in rule.

WHEN THE TARGET CHANGES
A three-day commitment may become a two-day commitment because work or caring
responsibilities changed. Change it deliberately and record when the new
target applies. Do not silently rewrite completed weeks to make the past look
better. Unknown old schedules should remain unknown rather than being filled
in with an invented seven-day expectation.

WHILE THE WEEK IS STILL OPEN
Two out of three on Thursday is progress, not a failed week. Check whether
there is a realistic remaining opportunity. At the end of the week, compare
the actual record with the target. Extra sessions do not pay next week's
commitment in advance.

USE THE WEEKLY REVIEW
Review one completed commitment, one unfinished commitment and the reason the
plan changed. Preserve the distinction between an appointment and proof of
action: a calendar entry means planned, not completed. A reminder is a prompt,
not evidence that the session happened.

FAQ
Does moving a habit to another day count?
In this weekly method, yes, if the same agreed action is completed within the
same week. A fixed appointment with another person still needs an agreed
change; flexibility is not permission to ignore arrangements.

Can a habit be three days a week instead of daily?
Yes. Define the weekly target and record the actual days. Use a daily rule
only when the responsibility genuinely calls for daily repetition.

What if I fall short?
Keep the record accurate. Review whether the commitment was realistic, what
prevented it and one adjustment. A missed target needs a decision, not a false
tick or a verdict on your whole character.
